Tresca Gen-1

Fylmer's trained cinematic model for carrying identity, visual language, timing, and sound across AI-generated shots.

Technical preview
by Fylmer

From prompts to production context

A coherent film is more than a sequence of good-looking generations. Every shot must inherit decisions from the story, the cast, the world, and the cut.

What we built

Tresca Gen-1 compiles shot direction into a production-aware generation workflow running on an on-demand GPU. It supports first- and last-frame conditioning, joint video and audio generation, explicit cut-or-continue modes, and per-user live progress.

Instead of asking a model to rediscover the same film on every prompt, Fylmer keeps the production contract attached: palette, lighting, identity, physical geography, camera behavior, timed action, and audio direction.

Continuity as a system

The reference workflow can bind identity and style images to the same request as a preceding video and its soundtrack. References are named and wired in a stable order, so the prompt and graph agree about which asset carries character, world, motion, or sound.

For frame-conditioned work, the previous clip's actual final frame can become the next shot's starting point. A separately chosen endpoint can also constrain where the shot must land. Titles and exact typography stay in post-production rather than being left to generative text rendering.

How Tresca Gen-1 was trained

Tresca Gen-1 is Fylmer's trained cinematic model layer, developed on top of our MiniMax H3 integration. Its first verified character adapter was trained with Fizgig on an A100 80 GB using six captioned 768-pixel clips for 60 epochs: 360 steps over 4 hours and 34 minutes, with video and audio latents trained together.

The final 296 MB Tresca Gen-1 adapter and its 20- and 40-epoch checkpoints are checksum-verified on Fylmer's persistent model-storage VPS.

Model foundation

Tresca Gen-1 is Fylmer's trained model layer built with MiniMax H3 as its generation foundation. MiniMax H3 remains an integration inside the Fylmer stack; Tresca Gen-1 is the model identity presented to creators.

Production study

Direkta is our working study in production coherence: a cast bible, locations, eight story beats, four visual candidates per beat, and assembled animatic passes. The point is not one perfect frame. It is the ability to inspect, compare, and carry decisions through a complete sequence.

Current limitations

Reference conditioning improves control but does not guarantee pixel-identical identity. Five-second clips must still be reviewed for anatomy, contact, camera continuity, audio joins, and endpoint fidelity. The system treats generated output as a take to direct and evaluate, not an automatic final cut.

GPU availability, model weights, network storage, and provider state remain operational dependencies. Fylmer checks balance before generation, reserves only one render at a time, and stops the on-demand pod after work completes, but provider-level spending controls are still the final protection.
